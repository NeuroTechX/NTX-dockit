Starting a Chapter
==================

A guide for a group starting, or restarting, a NeuroTechX chapter in its city.

Welcome
-------

NeuroTechX is an international non-profit community for neurotechnology. It began in 2015 with three chapters: Montréal, San Francisco and New York. A **chapter** is a group of volunteers who make their city a place where people interested in neurotechnology can find each other: students, researchers, engineers, clinicians, founders and hobbyists.

A chapter is run by its own organizers. NeuroTechX gives it a name people recognise, a network of other chapters, and open material to build on. We are a small volunteer-run organization, so this guide promises only what we can deliver today.

Our three pillars, in the order that works: **community** first, then **education**, then **innovation**. Bring people together, help them learn, and projects and companies follow.

What you get
------------

* **A listing** on `neurotechx.org/community <https://neurotechx.org/community>`__, with a link to wherever your chapter lives online.
* **A channel in the NeuroTechX Slack**, and an introduction to the other chapter organizers.
* **An announcement** of your launch in the NeuroTechX newsletter.
* **Your events** on `neurotechx.org/events <https://neurotechx.org/events>`__ when you send them to us.
* **The brand kit**: logos and templates, in the `Resource-Kit repository <https://github.com/NeuroTechX/Resource-Kit>`__.
* **Open-source projects to run events on**: `EEG-ExPy <https://github.com/NeuroTechX/EEG-ExPy>`__ (classic EEG experiments on consumer headsets), `MOABB <https://github.com/NeuroTechX/moabb>`__ (benchmarking for brain–computer interfaces), `awesome-bci <https://github.com/NeuroTechX/awesome-bci>`__ (a reading and tools list) and the `Neurotech Primer <https://neurotechx.org/primer>`__.
* **Hackathon support**: help planning and promoting a hackathon you run, and help with mentors, judges, prizes and devices.
* **Headset loans**: EEG headsets on loan for a workshop or a hackathon, on request.
* **Global NeuroHack 2027**: an invitation for your chapter to take part.
* **A support letter** from NeuroTechX, on request, for a venue, a university, a sponsor or a grant application.
* **A home for sponsorship money.** NeuroTechX is a registered non-profit. A sponsor of your chapter can pay NeuroTechX and get an invoice and a receipt. See "Money" below.

What we cannot offer today: a budget from NeuroTechX's own funds, a paid event-platform account, or devices to keep.

What we ask
-----------

#. **Hold at least one public event every quarter.** Regular events are what build a community.
#. **Follow the** `Code of Conduct <https://neurotechx.org/code-of-conduct>`__ and say so at your events. Reports go to `admins@neurotechx.com <mailto:admins@neurotechx.com>`__.
#. **Keep one contact address working**, and tell us when the organizers change.
#. **Answer the yearly check-in**: a short note on what you ran and who to contact.
#. **Stay with the science.** Present benefits, risks and unknowns. No pseudoscience and no medical claims.

Your first 90 days
------------------

**Weeks 1–2: say hello**

* Write to `chapters@neurotechx.com <mailto:chapters@neurotechx.com>`__ with your city, who you are, and what you have in mind.
* We set up a short call.
* Join the NeuroTechX Slack through `neurotechx.org/community <https://neurotechx.org/community>`__.

**Weeks 2–4: build the core team**

* Find two more organizers. Three people is the minimum that survives exams, job changes and holidays; four or five is comfortable.
* Split the work: one person for speakers and content, one for venue and logistics, one for announcements.
* Expect 5–10 hours a month each.

**Weeks 4–6: set up**

* **Name.** "NeuroTechX" followed by your city, for example "NeuroTechX Lisbon".
* **Home page.** One place people can find you and sign up for events. Use the event tool your city already uses, such as Luma, Eventbrite or Meetup. NeuroTechX no longer runs a Meetup network. The chapter's organizers own that account; keep at least two of you as administrators.
* **Contact address.** A shared mailbox that more than one organizer can read.
* **Venue.** Universities, company offices, maker spaces and libraries often host for free.
* Send us the link and the contact address. We list the chapter and open your Slack channel.

**Weeks 6–12: the first event**

* Pick something simple: two short talks and time to meet, or a live demo.
* Announce it four weeks ahead and again each week.
* Have a sign-up sheet at the door for people who want to hear about the next one.
* Send us the date. We announce it.

A year that works
-----------------

One event a quarter is the minimum. The first chapters settled on the fuller pattern below. Treat it as a guide, not a rule.

* **Three talks or networking evenings**, open to everyone. Good recurring topics: what is new in the field, brain–computer interfaces, brain stimulation, ethics.
* **Three hack nights**: small, hands-on, a laptop and a headset.
* **One workshop**: an introduction to EEG or to BCI for newcomers.

For each event:

* **Six weeks ahead:** theme, speakers, date.
* **Four weeks ahead:** venue booked, event page up.
* **Every week:** post in Slack and on your own channels.
* **On the day:** someone at the door, someone taking photos, someone hosting.
* **Afterwards:** a few lines and photos to the NeuroTechX Slack, so other chapters see it.

Free events lose about 40% of sign-ups on the day. Plan the room for that. Aim for 30 to 80 people.

Money
-----

* **NeuroTechX can receive sponsorship money for your chapter.** It is a registered non-profit. The sponsor pays NeuroTechX and gets an invoice and a receipt from us.
* **Write to us before you agree a sponsorship**, so we can issue the invoice and, if the sponsor wants one, a letter of support.
* **Spending it.** NeuroTechX either pays the venue or caterer directly or repays an organizer against receipts. We agree which when the sponsorship is set up. Keep every receipt.
* Do not collect sponsorship money personally, and do not sign an agreement or open an account in NeuroTechX's name.
* Small costs can stay simple: keep events free or at cost, and ask a venue or sponsor to pay the caterer directly.
* NeuroTechX is not a registered charity. Its receipts are business receipts, not charitable-donation tax receipts.
* Before you approach a company that already works with NeuroTechX, check with us, so that one sponsor does not hear from five chapters.

The name and the logo
---------------------

* Use the logo as it comes in the brand kit. Do not redraw or recolour it.
* You speak for your chapter, not for NeuroTechX as a whole.
* Keep your own company, lab or personal brand separate from the chapter's.
* If a chapter closes, its pages are taken down or handed to the next organizers.

Working with student clubs
--------------------------

If your city has university neurotech clubs, invite them in. Co-host an event, share speakers, and offer their graduates a place among your organizers. There is a separate `guide for student clubs <https://neurotechx.org/students/start-a-club>`__.

Who to write to
---------------

* **Starting a chapter, changes of organizer, events to announce:** `chapters@neurotechx.com <mailto:chapters@neurotechx.com>`__
* **Student clubs:** `studentclubs@neurotechx.com <mailto:studentclubs@neurotechx.com>`__
* **A Code of Conduct report:** `admins@neurotechx.com <mailto:admins@neurotechx.com>`__
* **Anything else:** `info@neurotechx.com <mailto:info@neurotechx.com>`__

In short
--------

What a chapter commits to:

#. We run at least one public event every quarter.
#. We follow and announce the NeuroTechX Code of Conduct.
#. We keep our contact address current and answer the yearly check-in.
#. We use the name and logo as given, and stop using them if the chapter closes or NeuroTechX asks.
#. Sponsorship goes through NeuroTechX: we write first, the sponsor pays NeuroTechX, and we sign nothing in NeuroTechX's name ourselves.
#. We present neurotechnology honestly.
